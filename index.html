Abstract

Nearby agents deform a local positive-definite metric. Inverse-metric descent steers each agent toward an unchanged goal. This approach encodes agent-agent interactions directly into the geometry of the space, rather than through explicit collision avoidance or force-based repulsion. The resulting trajectories emerge naturally from geodesic flow on the induced Riemannian manifold. We evaluate the method on standard crowd simulation benchmarks and compare against ORCA/RVO2 and Social Force baselines.

Method

Each agent induces a local positive-definite metric field g(x) in its neighborhood. The combined metric tensor at any point is the superposition of all agent-induced fields. Agents then follow inverse-metric descent:

ẋ = −g⁻¹(x) ∇V(x)

where V(x) is the goal potential. This guarantees geodesic-like paths that naturally avoid other agents through the metric deformation.

Full equations and metric definitions are in docs/scientific-core.md. The experiment protocol is detailed in docs/experiment-protocol.md.
