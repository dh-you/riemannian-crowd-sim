Abstract

Microscopic crowd controllers often encode avoidance either by adding repulsive interactions to goal-directed motion or by selecting a velocity from a collision-free set. In additive force and potential formulations, goal progress and avoidance can directly oppose one another, contributing to local minima, oscillation, and counterproductive reversals of motion. We propose instead an anisotropic Riemannian steering law in which nearby agents induce a local positive-definite metric and the commanded velocity is inverse-metric descent of an unchanged goal potential. This separates the navigation objective from the local descent geometry: the goal specifies where the agent should go, while the metric specifies which nearby directions have become costly. As a result, the commanded motion is uniquely defined, speed-bounded, and strictly decreases the goal potential. Held-out experiments show zero median pairwise penetration and completion of .84 and 1.00 in the dense corridor and bottleneck, respectively, compared with .61/.73 for ORCA and .47/.86 for JuPedSim.

Method

Agent $i$ has position $p_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$, velocity $v_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$, goal $q_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$, preferred speed $s_i > 0$, and goal tolerance $\varepsilon_q > 0$. Rather than adding a repulsive force to the goal vector, each neighbor $j$ raises the quadratic cost of radial approach more than tangential passage.

1. Anisotropic Agent Metric: For a non-coincident pair, define $r_{ij} = p_j - p_i$, $d_{ij} = \|r_{ij}\|$, and $\hat{r}_{ij} = r_{ij} / d_{ij}$. Agent $j$ contributes

$$A_{ij} = \lambda_t I + (\lambda_r - \lambda_t)\hat{r}_{ij}\hat{r}_{ij}^\top, \qquad 0 < \lambda_t < \lambda_r.$$

2. Motion-Conditioned Aggregation: Outside the goal region, let $h_i = (q_i - p_i) / \|q_i - p_i\|$ and $\dot{d}_{ij} = \hat{r}_{ij}^\top(v_j - v_i)$. With standard clamped cubic smoothstep $S_{[a,b]}$ and compactly supported Wendland $C^2$ kernel $\phi$, the closing gate $c_{ij}$, visibility gate $f_{ij}$, and effective metric $G_i$ are

$$c_{ij} = S_{[0, s_i]}(-\dot{d}_{ij}), \qquad f_{ij} = S_{[-1, 1]}(h_i^\top \hat{r}_{ij}), \qquad G_i = I + \alpha \sum_{j \neq i} \phi\!\left(\frac{d_{ij}}{\sigma}\right) c_{ij} f_{ij} A_{ij}.$$

3. Inverse-Metric Steering: Let $g_i = q_i - p_i$ and solve $G_i u_i = g_i$. The commanded velocity is Riemannian steepest descent of $\Phi_i(x) = \frac{1}{2}\|x - q_i\|^2$ normalized to metric speed $s_i$:

$$v_i^\star = \begin{cases} s_i\, u_i \big/ \sqrt{u_i^\top G_i u_i}, & \|g_i\| > \varepsilon_q, \\ 0, & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$

Since $G_i \succeq I$, $\|v_i^\star\| \le s_i$ and $\nabla \Phi_i(p_i)^\top v_i^\star = -s_i \sqrt{g_i^\top G_i^{-1} g_i} < 0$ outside the goal region: interactions redirect descent without canceling it into a false equilibrium away from the target.

4. Integration and Correction: With step $\Delta t$, smoothing time $\tau > 0$, and $\beta = 1 - e^{-\Delta t / \tau}$,

$$\bar{v}_i^{n+1} = (1 - \beta)v_i^n + \beta v_i^{\star,n}, \qquad \tilde{p}_i^{n+1} = p_i^n + \Delta t\,\bar{v}_i^{n+1}, \qquad p^{n+1} = \mathcal{P}(\tilde{p}^{n+1}),$$

where $\mathcal{P}$ applies fixed agent-separation and wall-projection sweeps.

Experimental Evaluation

We compare the proposed controller (Ours, $\alpha=20$, $\sigma=1.5\,\text{m}$, $\lambda_r/\lambda_t=20$, $\tau=0.04\,\text{s}$) with Euclidean goal steering under identical smoothing and two-sweep positional correction (Goal+Proj.), ORCA/RVO2, and JuPedSim 1.4.2 SocialForceModel across five held-out settings: perturbed two-agent head-on and perpendicular-crossing encounters, a 50-agent antipodal circle, two opposing 60-agent corridor streams, and a 112-agent bottleneck.
